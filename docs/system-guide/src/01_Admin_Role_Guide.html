1 Role at a glance

Admin is the highest of three roles (Admin, HR Manager, HR User). An admin is the organization's gatekeeper: nobody can use the platform unless an admin has invited or approved them, and only an admin can create teams, choose managers, change roles, or deactivate accounts.

Scope

The whole organization: every team, every user, every sourcing file. The admin reads all of it, but writes only to their own jobs and candidates.

Main workplaces

/admin for people and teams, /manager for one team's dashboard, /reports for org-wide or per-team reports, plus the normal personal pages.

Can see / Can do / Cannot do

CAN SEE

  • Every user, role, team, status, last login and last activity
  • Org-wide pipeline: New, Reviewed, Shortlisted, Contacted, Hired, Rejected
  • Status of every sourcing run (running, completed, failed)
  • Every team's dashboard, member pages and sourcing files
  • Any job, candidate, score, note and status history, opened read-only
  • The latest 20 recorded actions across the organization
  • Pending invites and access requests
  • Org-wide or per-team reports (4 types)
  • Own personal dashboard, jobs and candidates

CAN DO

  • Invite a person with a role and team
  • Approve an access request (choose role and team)
  • Withdraw an unused invite
  • Change a user's role (Admin, HR Manager, HR User)
  • Move a user to a team or to "No team"
  • Deactivate and reactivate accounts
  • Create teams
  • Assign or clear a team's manager
  • Export reports (CSV, Excel) and any readable job (Excel)
  • Create and manage own jobs and searches, like any user

CANNOT DO

  • Reject or delete an access request
  • Delete a user or edit an email address
  • Demote or deactivate their own account
  • Rename or delete a team from the UI
  • Edit, delete, re-score, re-run or annotate anyone else's jobs and candidates
  • Resend an invite (no email is ever sent)
  • Reset a password or force a sign-out
  • Be a team's manager (only an active HR Manager can)
  • Undo an action with a button: there is no undo or confirmation
Identity in one linePeople are identified by email only. The display name shown everywhere is derived from the part before the @, split on . - _ and Title-Cased: zeyad.ragab@company.com appears as "Zeyad Ragab".

3 Signing in and being the last line of access

The access model

Access is a single fact: the person has an active profile. There is no email allowlist any more. Anyone can create an account or use Google sign-in, but without an active profile they are signed straight back out.

Middleware ruleProtected pages (/dashboard /jobs /candidates /settings /admin /manager /reports): not signed in goes to /login?redirectTo=...; signed in without an active profile is signed out and sent to /login?error=not_allowed.
API and database ruleEvery API call checks the caller: no user or inactive profile gives 401 Unauthorized, wrong role 403 Forbidden. Admin and manager powers in the database also require an active profile.
Message the person sees"Your account doesn't have access yet. Ask an admin to invite you or approve your access request."
After password sign-up"Account created. Check your email to confirm, then log in. If you weren't invited, an admin will need to approve your access first." (This email is Supabase's confirmation mail; the platform itself sends none.)

The four account states

StateHow it is storedWhat the person experiencesWhere you see it
InvitedA row in the invites table (email, role, team). No account exists.On first sign-in (Google or password sign-up) the account is created already active with the invited role and team.Access & Invitations, "Pending invites"
Access requestInactive profile, role HR User, no team.Signs up or signs in without an invite: immediately signed out with "not_allowed". Waits until approved."Access requests" list, badge count, bell alert

Edge case to rememberAn access request is defined as "inactive + HR User + no team". A deactivated HR User who has no team matches that definition, so they appear under Access requests (with an Approve button) and are hidden from User Management, User Performance and reports. Approving them is how you reactivate them.

5 Button and control reference

All toasts appear top-right for 4 seconds and can be closed. If an action fails, the toast shows the API's message; fallbacks are "The change could not be saved." and, for connection problems, "Network error — the change could not be saved." Buttons are disabled while their own request is in flight. None of the admin controls ask for confirmation.

6 Step-by-step tasks

6.1 Onboard a person you have invited

  1. Open Admin, scroll to Access & Invitations.
  2. Type the person's email (lower or upper case, it is normalised).
  3. Pick the Role and the Team (check it: the default may be wrong).
  4. Click Invite. Toast: "{email} can sign in as {Role}."
  5. The person appears under Pending invites. Tell them yourself to go to the login page and use Google or create an email and password account. No email is sent.
  6. On their first sign-in they become active with the invited role and team, and the invite disappears from the list.
Behind the scenesA password sign-up needs Supabase's email confirmation first, then a normal login. Re-inviting the same email overwrites the earlier invite (this is how you edit one). If you invite an HR Manager to a team, they replace that team's current manager when they arrive.

6.2 Approve an access request

  1. A bell notification "{Name} requested access to the app" or the badge "{n} request(s)" tells you someone is waiting.
  2. In Access requests, read the email and "Tried to sign in {time}". Confirm you recognise the person.
  3. Set the row's Role and Team.
  4. Click Approve. Toast: "{Name} can now sign in as {Role}."
  5. Tell the person to sign in again.
There is no RejectTo decline a request, do nothing: it stays in the list indefinitely. Never approve an unknown email just to clear the list.

6.3 Create a team and assign a manager

  1. In the Teams card type the name in "New team name" and click Add Team. Toast: Created team "{name}".
  2. Make sure the future manager exists as an active HR Manager. If not, promote them first in User Management (6.5) or invite them as HR Manager.
  3. In the new team's row, open the Manager dropdown and choose the person. Toast: "{Name} now manages {Team}."
  4. Add team members by choosing the team in each user's Team select (6.6), or by inviting/approving with that team selected.
  5. Reload and check the row: Manager filled, Members counts correct.
Behind the scenesChoosing a manager moves that person into the team and clears them as manager of any other team. If the team already had a manager, the new choice replaces it, but the previous manager stays an HR Manager in the same team.

6.4 Promote or demote a user

  1. In User Management find the person (sorted by name).
  2. Open the Role select and pick Admin, HR Manager or HR User. It saves instantly.
  3. Toast: "{name} is now {Role}." The person's access changes on their next request; no message is sent to them.
Behind the scenes (manager sync)

To HR Manager with a team and no current manager: they become the team's manager. From HR Manager: they are cleared as manager of every team. To Admin: full read access to the organization at once. You cannot change your own role.

6.5 Move a user between teams

  1. In User Management, open the person's Team select.
  2. Choose the new team (or "No team"). Toast: "Moved {name} to {Team}." or "Removed {name} from their team."
  3. Check the Teams table for the effect on both teams' manager and member counts.
Behind the scenesThe user's jobs and candidates stay owned by them and follow them: the old team's manager loses sight of them and the new team's manager gains it. A moved HR Manager is cleared as manager of teams they left; if the new team has no manager they become its manager, otherwise they are a co-manager by role only (both see the team, but the Manager column shows only the first).

6.6 Deactivate and reactivate

  1. In User Management click Deactivate on the row. It happens immediately: toast "Deactivated {name}." The badge turns to "Inactive".
  2. The person's API calls fail at once; on their next page load they are signed out with "not_allowed". Their data stays and still counts in totals.
  3. To restore, click Reactivate (toast "Reactivated {name}.") or re-invite them through the Invite form to set a new role and team in the same step.
Watch outIf the person is an HR User with no team, they will not appear in User Management after deactivation. They reappear under Access requests. Use Approve there to restore them.

6.7 Reassign or clear a manager

  1. In the Teams table open the Manager dropdown of the team.
  2. Pick another active HR Manager to replace the current one, or Unassigned to clear it.
  3. Check the outcome. The previous manager keeps the HR Manager role and their team membership, so they can still see the whole team. To remove their oversight, change their role or move them (6.4, 6.5).

6.8 Review a team's work through /manager

  1. Click Team in the nav, or a team-name link in the Teams table. If you have several teams, use the Team picker and View team.
  2. Read the banner: team name, "Managed by {name}" (or "No manager assigned"), active members. The chip says "Admin view · Team".
  3. Check the four cards (Team Members active/total, Team Jobs, Average Match Quality, Shortlisted Talent), Team Pipeline and Sourcing Runs.
  4. Use Team Sourcing Files and its filters to find work; click View to open a file.
  5. Use Member Comparison and Team Activity for people and recent actions.

"Unseen by you" in this page's Sourcing Runs card and Unseen column count candidates you have not opened, not what the team has seen.

6.9 Open a member's file (read-only)

  1. On /admin, click a user's name in User Performance, or in /manager click a member card.
  2. On the member page review the Status, Last login, cards, pipeline and Sourcing Files (up to 50) and "Recently Opened" (last 8 files they opened).
  3. Click View on a file. The candidates page opens with the banner "Viewing {Owner}'s sourcing file · read-only".

6.10 Run reports and export

  1. Click Reports in the nav.
  2. Pick the Scope ("Whole organization" or a team) and the Period, then Update report.
  3. Switch the report type tab as needed (section 8).
  4. Click CSV or Excel. The file downloads as {title}-report-{scope}-{YYYY-MM-DD}.{ext}.

7 Team and manager rules explained

Two facts kept in step

1. Who can see a team's data is decided by the database from the user's role = HR Manager, active, and team_id = that team. 2. Who is shown as "the manager" is the team's manager_id field. The admin screens keep the two consistent, but they are updated in separate steps and can drift.

One manager, one team

  • A team has at most one shown manager; a person can be shown as manager of at most one team.
  • Only an active HR Manager can be chosen. An admin can never be a team's manager.
  • More than one HR Manager can sit in the same team. All of them see the team's data and get its notifications, but the Manager column shows only one.

Consequences worth knowing

EventResult
Deactivate an HR ManagerTeams show "Unassigned". They lose all manager powers instantly (the database ignores inactive profiles).
Reactivate an HR ManagerThey regain their powers. They are re-shown as manager only if their team has no manager.
Set "Unassigned" on a teamCosmetic for oversight: the former manager still leads the team by role and team membership. Notifications keep flowing to them.
Invite or Approve as HR Manager with a teamThe person is placed in the team and becomes its manager, replacing the current manager. If the person is not a valid active HR Manager, the manager step is skipped silently.
Create a team with a manager via the APINot offered in the UI. (If the API is called with an invalid manager, the team is still created.)
Admin placed on a teamAllowed by the UI. They are then counted as a team member in the team dashboard and totals.

Not availableThere is no export of users, the activity log, or the Team Sourcing Files table, and no bulk user import. Access requests are excluded from every report scope.

9 Read-only access to others' work and how access is recorded

The admin can open anybody's job, candidates, scores, notes and status history by following a link (View buttons, member pages, notifications). Data is visible because the database grants admins read access to every sourcing table; it has no write access to them.

What the admin sees on someone else's file

  • An indigo banner: "Viewing {Owner}'s sourcing file · read-only" (falls back to "a teammate's").
  • Candidate status appears as a plain badge, not a dropdown.
  • "Score Candidates" and delete buttons are hidden.
Server enforcementEven if a request is forged, edits are refused with 403: "You can view this {job|candidate}, but only its owner can change it." This applies to editing or deleting a job, running a search, scoring, changing status, adding notes, and editing or deleting a candidate.

How opening is recorded

The log line reads Opened {Owner}'s sourcing file "{title}", labelled "Opened a teammate's sourcing file". Your own opens of other people's files are therefore visible to other admins in Recent Activity and reports. In the Team Sourcing Files table, the "Last Accessed By" column shows the latest opener with a "Not owner" tag when it is not the owner.

Other things you can and cannot seeYou can export any job you can open (Excel, 20 per minute). You cannot see other users' "unseen" state: it is per viewer, so any "Unseen" figure you see is about your own opens.

12 Limits and safety

What an admin cannot do

LimitationExactly how it behaves
No self-demotion or self-deactivationRole select and Deactivate are disabled on your own row. The API refuses with 400 "You can't remove your own admin access. Ask another admin." You can still change your own team.
No "last admin" checkThe only protection is the self-rule. One admin can demote or deactivate another admin, including the only other admin. Two admins acting on each other at the same moment could leave zero admins.
No confirmation dialogsDeactivate, Withdraw, role and team changes all apply at the first click or selection.
No reject or delete for access requestsOnly Approve exists. Unapproved requests stay listed.
No delete user, edit email, password reset, force sign-outDeactivation is the only way to block a person. A deactivated user is signed out on their next page load, not instantly on the open page.
No emails sentThe platform sends no email of its own. Invitees and affected users are told nothing. Invites have no resend and no expiry.
Team names not unique; no rename or delete in the UIDuplicate names are accepted. Mistakes cannot be corrected from the interface.
Team creation has no manager pickerAssign the manager afterwards from the Manager dropdown.
Manager choices are limitedOnly active HR Managers appear. Admins cannot be managers.
Cannot write to others' workNo edit, delete, re-score, re-run, status change or notes on files owned by someone else.
Not atomicUser updates run in steps. A failure in the manager-sync step returns "Failed to update team manager." even though the profile change already took effect. Refresh and check both the user row and the team row.
Admin routes are not rate limitedRepeated clicks send repeated requests.

Practical advice

Tip Keep at least two active admins you trust. You cannot demote yourself, and nobody guards against demoting the other admin. Never deactivate another admin without confirming that a third person still has admin access.
Tip Before clicking Invite or Approve, re-read the Role and Team selects on that row. The Team default may be the first team, not the one you intend, and inviting an HR Manager to a team replaces that team's manager. Type or paste emails carefully: a typo creates an invite nobody can use, which sits in Pending invites until you Withdraw it.
Tip After any invite, approval, deactivation or role change, tell the person yourself. They receive nothing from the platform.
Tip Give teams distinct, descriptive names (for example "Sourcing - Cairo") because they cannot be renamed or removed from the UI and duplicates look identical.
Tip Deactivate rather than "removing": their data, jobs and history stay intact for audit. Give a team-less user a team before deactivating them so they remain in User Management. After any manager change, reload /admin and check the Teams table for a team left without a manager.
