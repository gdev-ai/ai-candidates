1. What the system is and who it is for

A web application that helps recruiters find candidates in Egypt. You upload or paste a job description, AI turns it into structured requirements, the system searches public sources for matching people, and AI scores each person against the role.

1. Describe the role

Upload a PDF/DOCX/TXT or paste text. AI extracts title, skills, seniority, education and more. You review and edit them.

2. Find people

AI writes Google X-ray search queries. SerpApi runs them, Apify enriches LinkedIn profiles, and only candidates located in Egypt are kept.

3. Score and decide

On demand, AI scores every candidate 0-100 against the job. Recruiters set statuses, add notes and export to Excel.

Who uses it

PersonWhat they do in the system
HR UserThe recruiter. Creates jobs, runs sourcing searches, scores and manages candidates. Sees only their own work.
HR ManagerLeads a team of HR Users. Does everything an HR User does for their own work, plus a read-only view of the team: performance, sourcing files, candidates and activity. Gets notifications about team activity.
AdminRuns the organization. Invites and approves people, creates teams, assigns roles and managers, deactivates access, sees organization-wide performance and all reports. Also read-only on other people's sourcing data.

Key ideas to remember

  • Access is by invitation. Signing up is not enough. An account only works when an admin has invited or approved it (section 4).
  • Data has one owner. Whoever creates a job owns it and everything found for it. Nobody else can edit it, not even an admin. Managers and admins can only look (sections 2 and 8).
  • Searching and scoring are separate steps. A search finds candidates but does not score them. Scoring is a manual click on the candidates page (section 7).
  • Everything is recorded. Important actions go into an append-only activity log that feeds notifications and reports (section 10).
  • Egypt only. Every job is located in Egypt; the optional city narrows the search to Cairo, Alexandria, Giza or Suez.
Vocabulary used throughout

Job = a role you are hiring for. Sourcing run (also called a sourcing file) = one execution of a search for a job. Candidate = a person found. Match score = AI's 0-100 fit rating of a candidate for a specific job. See the Glossary in section 15.

2. The three roles at a glance

Roles form a hierarchy of visibility, but not of ownership. Going up the tiers you can see more, yet you can never edit another person's jobs or candidates.

Admin

Default landing: /dashboard (personal). Extra pages: /admin, /manager (any team), org-wide /reports. Bell: access requests only.

HR Manager

4. Getting in: accounts and access

Nobody can use the app just by creating an account. Access equals an active profile in the database. An admin grants it either before the person signs in (an invite) or after (approving an access request).

Account lifecycle

Path A: invited first

  1. Admin enters email, role and team on /admin and clicks Invite.
  2. The system stores a pending invite. No email is sent; tell the person yourself.
  3. The person signs up (email or Google) using that exact email.
  4. A database trigger creates an active profile with the invited role and team, and removes the invite.

Path B: request first

  1. Someone signs up or signs in with Google without an invite.
  2. An inactive profile is created; they are signed out with "not_allowed".
  3. All admins get a bell notification linking to /admin.
  4. Admin picks role and team and clicks Approve. The person can now sign in.
Edge case

An access request is recognised as "inactive + no team + role HR User". A deactivated HR User who has no team looks identical, so they appear under Access requests instead of User Management.

Deactivation

An admin clicks Deactivate on /admin. The person keeps all their data, but immediately every API call returns 401, and at their next page load they are signed out with the "not_allowed" message. Any elevated database powers (admin or manager visibility) also stop at once. If they were a team's manager, the team becomes "Unassigned". Reactivating restores access; an HR Manager with a team is re-attached as manager only if that team currently has none.

What does NOT notify

File opens, job creation or edits, notes, status changes to Reviewed / Contacted / Rejected, sign-ins, a manager's own actions, and anything for a person without a team. Admins are notified only about access requests, not team activity. There are no emails or push messages anywhere in the system.

Toasts, errors and loading

  • Toasts appear top-right for 4 seconds in four colours (success, error, warning, info) and each has a close button. They confirm saves and report failures.
  • Something went wrong page: shown for unexpected errors, with a Try again button.
  • Page not found page with a Back to dashboard link. Also used when you open a team member outside your team.
  • Skeleton loaders (grey pulsing blocks) appear while Dashboard, Admin, Team, Reports and Settings load.

Settings (/settings) and logging out

Settings is open to every role and contains one card, "Account", showing "Signed in as <your email>". There are no editable controls: no password change, profile or preferences.

Log out (outline button in the menu, no confirmation): records a "Signed out" audit event, ends the session and returns to /login.

What happens when you click "Generate Queries & Find Candidates"

  1. AI writes 2-4 Google X-ray search queries from the requirements.
  2. The job is saved to your account (before any search starts).
  3. A sourcing run is created and the search runs in the background; the page gets a 202 reply.
  4. The page polls the run status every 2 seconds for up to 10 minutes. On completion a dialog shows the number of candidates found. If it is still running after 10 minutes you see "Search is taking longer than expected. Check back shortly." and the search continues on the server.
Wording vs. reality

The progress label "computing AI match scores" and the dialog phrase "identified and evaluated N candidates" are misleading: no scoring has happened yet. Scores appear only after you click "Score Candidates" on the candidates page. Also, if the search fails to start, the job stays saved and clicking again creates a duplicate job. You cannot see or edit the generated queries.

7. The AI sourcing pipeline end to end

From a pasted job description to a shortlist in fifteen steps. Blue steps are yours, purple use OpenAI, amber call outside services. Note the break between step 12 and 13: searching never scores. Scoring is a separate, manual click.

8. Ownership, access tracking and read-only viewing

Four words describe who touched a sourcing file. They appear as columns on the dashboard and the Team pages, and they are easy to confuse.

Created by

The person who started the sourcing run (stored as the run's created_by_email). Shown as "File Created By" or "by <starter>". Older runs may have none.

Owned by

The owner of the job the run belongs to. Only the owner can change the job, its candidates, scores, statuses and notes. Ownership never transfers, even when someone else opens the file.

Accessed by

Every time someone opens the candidate list with a runId, a row is written to the access table. "Last Accessed By" shows the latest row, with a "Not owner" tag on Team pages when the opener is not the owner.

Managed by

The team's manager, shown in the Team hero ("Managed by ...") and the Manager column on /admin. Managers can see their team's data because of their role and team, not because of this label.

What opening a teammate's file does

  1. A Manager or Admin clicks View on a file (or follows a run notification). The URL is /candidates?jobId=...&runId=....
  2. The page asks the server to record the access. A row is added every time (also for owners opening their own file).
  3. If the opener is not the owner and has not opened this run in the last 30 minutes, the activity log also gets "Opened <owner>'s sourcing file".
  4. The job reports that you cannot edit it, so the page switches to read-only: a banner "Viewing <Owner>'s sourcing file - read-only" appears, Score Candidates and delete buttons vanish, and statuses become plain badges.
  5. Filters, sorting, pagination, candidate profiles and Excel export still work.
Enforced in three layers

Hidden buttons are only cosmetic. The API also refuses every write from a non-owner with 403 "You can view this job, but only its owner can change it.", and the database's row-level security has no write policy for anyone else. Not even an Admin can edit another person's data.

Other things to know

  • Unseen is per viewer. A candidate becomes "seen" for you only when you open the profile with a job context. A manager's Unseen count says nothing about what the owner has seen.
  • Opening via the Jobs page, the picker, or job-scored notifications carries no runId, so no access is recorded.
  • Recently Opened links on a member's page record an access by you, not by the member.
  • Opening a file does not create a notification. It does appear in the team Activity feed and the Activity report ("Teammate files opened").
  • Managers' and admins' Excel exports of a teammate's job are not logged.
  • HR Users cannot open other people's jobs at all (the API returns 404).

10. Activity log and audit trail

Every meaningful action writes one row to the activity_log table: who, what, on which record, a readable description and extra details. The log powers the Recent Activity feeds, the Activity report and the manager notifications.

Who can see it

  • HR User only their own rows. HR Manager rows of their team's members. Admin all rows.
  • Feeds show the newest 20 (admin, team), or 25 on a member page. Descriptions read as "<Actor> <description>" with times such as "2 hours ago".
  • There is no screen to search the log or export it.

Immutability

Append-only

The table has no update or delete policy for anyone. Rows can only be added, and an insert must carry the caller's own user id. Failures to write a log entry are recorded in server logs but never block the action itself. Notifications are generated from log rows by a database trigger and only their "read" time can be changed by the recipient.

12. Security model

Layers of protection

1. Middleware (route guard)

Protects /dashboard /jobs /candidates /settings /admin /manager /reports. No session goes to /login; a session without an active profile is signed out and sent to /login?error=not_allowed. The home page, login, auth callback and /api/* are not covered here.

2. Page checks

Server pages re-check the role: /admin redirects non-admins to the dashboard, /manager redirects HR Users, and the team member page returns "not found" outside the manager's team.

3. API guards

Every route calls requireUser (401 if no user or profile inactive). Admin routes use requireRole(["admin"]) (403). Writes call the owner check (403). Bodies are validated with zod (400). Some routes are rate limited.

4. Row Level Security

The database itself filters rows for every query, regardless of what the app asks for. It is the last line of defense and cannot be bypassed from the browser.

Deactivation reaches the database

The role helper functions ignore inactive profiles, so a deactivated admin or manager immediately loses elevated data access, not only app access. The original hard-coded email allow-list was removed; only four bootstrap accounts were kept active when access control moved into the database.

Other protections

  • Secrets: the service-role key and provider keys stay on the server. Errors returned to users are safe generic messages; every response carries an X-Request-Id for support.
  • Sign-in abuse: failed-login recording is rate limited per IP and per account and never confirms whether an email exists.
  • CSV injection: exported cells starting with = + - @, tab or carriage return are neutralised.
  • Export and AI limits protect cost and stability (section 7). The limiter is per server instance, in memory.
  • No email is ever sent by the system, including invites; nothing to phish or misdeliver, but people must be told out of band.

13. Common workflows

A. Recruiter runs a search HR User

  1. Open Jobs → New Job (or the dashboard button).
  2. Fill Job Title, Company, Employment Type, Work Arrangement.
  3. Upload the job description (PDF, DOCX, TXT) or paste at least 50 characters.
  4. Click Analyze Job with AI and wait for "Requirements successfully extracted".
  5. Review the requirements: fix seniority, pick a City if the role is city-specific, trim or add skills, education and keywords.
  6. Click Generate Queries & Find Candidates. Do not click again if it fails after "Saving role": the job already exists, so check Jobs first.
  7. Wait for the "Candidate Sourcing Complete!" dialog and click Show Candidates.
  8. Click Score Candidates and wait for the toast. Sort by Match Score.
  9. Open promising people, set statuses (Reviewed, Shortlisted...), add notes.
  10. Use Export Excel to share the shortlist (apply filters first, for example Status = Shortlisted).

B. Manager reviews a teammate's file HR Manager

  1. Click the bell if you have notifications, or open Team in the menu.
  2. On Team Sourcing Files filter by member, status, dates or minimum match.
  3. Click View on the file. Your access is recorded and the page opens read-only.
  4. Sort by Match Score, open candidates with the eye icon to read scores, strengths, concerns and notes.
  5. Use Export Excel if you need a copy.
  6. To give feedback or ask for changes, talk to the owner: you cannot change statuses, notes or scores.
  7. For a broader view use Team → Member page and Reports (team scope).

C. Admin onboards a person Admin

  1. Open Admin → Access & Invitations.
  2. Enter the person's exact work email, pick Role and Team (pick a team first if needed; see workflow D).
  3. Click Invite. A green toast confirms.
  4. Tell the person yourself to open the app and sign up (email and password, or Google) with that exact email. Nothing is emailed.
  5. They confirm their email if using a password, then log in. They arrive on the dashboard with the role and team you chose.
  6. If they signed in before you invited them, they are in Access requests: choose role and team and click Approve (or invite them, which activates them at once).
  7. To remove access later, use Deactivate in User Management.

D. Admin creates a team and assigns a manager Admin

  1. In the Teams card type the name next to "New team name" and click Add Team.
  2. Make sure the future manager exists with the role HR Manager and is active (Invite as HR Manager, or change their role in User Management).
  3. In the Teams table open the Manager dropdown for the new team and pick them. They are moved into the team automatically and removed as manager of any other.
  4. Move the team's recruiters into it using the Team dropdown in User Management. Their existing work moves with them.
  5. Click the team name to open the team dashboard and check members, then confirm the manager sees a Team link and the bell.

14. Known limitations and gotchas

An honest list of behaviours a new owner should know about. None stop the system from working, but each can surprise people.

Sourcing and scoring

GotchaDetails
Search does not scoreThe UI says "computing AI match scores" and "evaluated N candidates", but scores exist only after clicking Score Candidates. Dashboards show "No evaluated files yet" until then.
Job is saved before the searchIf starting the search fails (for example the 5/min limit), the job stays and clicking again creates a duplicate job.
Queries are invisibleGenerated queries are stored but never shown, and cannot be edited. The SearchQueries editor component exists but is unused.
Score has no formulaThe overall match score is whatever the AI returns. A fractional score would fail to save that candidate (reported as a failure).
Score button re-scores everythingNo confirmation, overwrites earlier scores, costs one AI call per candidate and runs synchronously with no time limit configured.
Non-durable background searchRuns after the HTTP reply with no retry. If the server stops it, the run can stay "running" forever; nothing cleans it up. The page stops watching after 10 minutes.
Egypt filter is text-basedCandidates whose location lacks "Egypt" or a governorate name (e.g. "Alex") are dropped.
Default search provider is mockUnless SEARCH_PROVIDER=serpapi, results are 8 fixture people. serper throws "not implemented".
Complete runs can carry errorsA "complete" run may hold a note (failed enrichment, link errors) that the UI does not display.
Employment Type / Title duplicatedThe requirements editor's copies of these are ignored for the saved job once the Job Details section is filled.
Scanned PDFsProduce empty text; Analyze stays disabled with no explanation. The 10 MB check is server-side only, and hosting limits may reject large uploads earlier.

Candidates and data

GotchaDetails
Candidate delete is globalDeleting removes the candidate from every job, with notes, history and matches, though the toast says "removed from this job".
Status is globalA candidate's status is shared across all jobs they are linked to.
No confirmationsStatus changes, Score Candidates, Approve, Deactivate, Withdraw, role and team changes and Log out all act on first click. Only deleting a job or candidate asks.
Picker shows 20 jobsThe Candidates job picker lists only your 20 newest jobs and has no pager; use the Jobs page for older ones.
Dashboard "today" linksCandidate links in "Today's Searched Candidates" open the profile without job context (no Match card, not marked seen).
Export is a plain linkErrors (like a 429) navigate to raw JSON. Filters typed but not applied are not exported.
Status change on profileCan hide the Match Score card and edit controls until reload.
Notes are permanentCannot be edited, deleted, and do not show the author.
Average match differsThe average excludes scores without a run id, while the distribution chart includes all scores.
Shortlisted countsDashboard shortlisted counts ignore Hired and Contacted.

Access and administration

GotchaDetails
No reject for access requestsAdmins can only Approve. Unwanted requests stay listed forever.
No emails or noticesInvites, approvals, role changes and deactivations are not communicated to the person.
Deactivated lookalikeA deactivated HR User with no team is shown as an access request.
No last-admin guardYou cannot demote or deactivate yourself, but you can do it to another admin, even the last other one.
Team management is thinNo rename or delete team in the UI; duplicate team names are allowed; Add Team has no manager picker; "Unassigned" keeps the old manager's team and permissions.
Multi-step changes are not atomicUser and team updates run several statements; a failure midway can leave partial changes, and creating a team with an invalid manager returns an error but leaves the team.
Invite defaultsThe Team dropdown's default is fixed when the page first renders; teams created later require a reload. Invites never expire; a mistyped email sits until withdrawn.
Sign-in redirectThe redirectTo set by the route guard is ignored by password login; you always land on the dashboard.
Settings are emptyOnly shows your email. No password change or profile editing.
Companies have no UIChanged only in the database.

Platform

GotchaDetails
In-memory rate limiterEach server instance counts separately, so limits are soft on a serverless host and reset on restart. Admin routes are not limited at all.
Time zones"Today" and report windows use the server clock (UTC on Vercel), not the viewer's.
Team dashboards are snapshotsNo auto-refresh; caps of 200 files (500 with filters) apply to team KPIs, 20 items to activity feeds and notifications.
Manager sees only their teamA manager without a team, or an inactive one, sees nothing of the team.
